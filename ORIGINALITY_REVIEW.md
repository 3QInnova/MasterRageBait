# Originality review and design revision

Date: October 2, 2026. Scope: targeted web searches and official storefront/developer descriptions; no hands-on competitor testing, exhaustive catalog search, asset audit, or legal clearance.

## Finding

The earlier concept was too familiar to confidently market as distinctive. Charge jumps, vertical progress loss, randomized traps, and sarcastic commentary are established design patterns. Combining them with a delivery robot and a new tower does not by itself create a strong identity.

No finished code or art exists in this project to audit for copying. This review concerns the proposed design. The design can be developed independently, but similarity research cannot certify that nothing comparable exists.

## Distinctive gameplay proposal

**You can lock one malfunction into a useful piece of the level.** A random hazard becomes a player-selected route modification. Keeping it locked makes return climbs easier; moving the clamp to a later fault releases the earlier shortcut. Other events remain unpredictable, including attempt-ending surprises.

Example: a sign unexpectedly swings through a jump path. After surviving it, the player clamps it horizontally to form a shortcut. Higher up, a ventilation flap offers a better shortcut. Moving the clamp there frees the sign below; a later fall makes the return climb different again.

Randomness chooses the problem. Player choice changes the geometry. That relationship should be visible in the first playable room, rather than appearing as an optional late-game ability.

Targeted searches around repair, trapping, freezing hazards, and tower platformers did not establish an exact match to this proposed loop. They also did not establish uniqueness. Broader playtesting and competitor inspection remain useful once the prototype has concrete behavior.

## Independent creation requirements

- Write movement and event code for this project; do not lift competitor code or reverse-engineered implementations.
- Draw new room layouts and obstacle sequences. Do not reproduce recognizable levels, then change their colors.
- Give the character a new silhouette and animation language. Do not reuse recognizable protagonists, music, dialogue, UI, or visual compositions from the reference games.
- Maintain asset provenance as assets are introduced: creator, source, license, and permitted use. Use original or appropriately licensed material.
- Rewrite the floor-four parcel switch as a maintenance bypass. Keep functional game text consistent with the new objective.
- Research the final release name separately. MasterRageBait is currently an internal folder label; it has not been cleared as a brand.

## Prototype evidence needed

The first room must demonstrate a random malfunction, a useful clamped state, and a meaningful choice about where to use the clamp. Compare play with and without that mechanic. Ask testers to describe the game's defining action without suggesting the answer.

If testers describe only jumping and random traps, the differentiation has not landed. If they independently describe using malfunctions to build a route and the cost of moving the clamp, we have evidence of a more recognizable identity. Neither outcome establishes legal clearance or worldwide uniqueness.
